Each track shows one whole arrangement — a neighbourhood some set of genomes actually has, not a position-by-position consensus — read outward from the focal locus in its own direction of transcription, so A-1 is the gene immediately upstream whichever strand it sits on.

  • Transparency — for each locus, how many genomes its part of the graph reaches. Not every locus reaches the whole pangenome.
  • Colour — in contrast, how the divergent genes at a position divide it: deep blue where this one always holds it, maroon where several compete. 20 in 100 is an ordinary reading, not a poor one.
  • Click a gene to walk to it. Onto one transcribed the other way, the frame reverses: A+1 becomes A-1, so you carry on along the stream.
  • Click the bar to see how all member genes split at that position. Under the focal gene it is the sequence families filling this locus.
  • White blocks are the intergenic regions — the space between two genes, named for the RNA gene, origin of replication or CRISPR array Bakta found in it, or by the genes that bracket it. A white block is not a Nuna locus. It has no cluster and no embedding, it is not in the locus count, and you cannot walk to it; its width is a median across genomes that disagree. They are white rather than coloured precisely because the divergence measure that colours a gene does not exist for them.
  • ·1504 — the locus id. Names repeat (tnp on hundreds); search an id, or put it after # in the address bar.

What a locus is

A pangenome tool built on sequence identity defines a gene family as sequences similar enough to each other, and so has a floor — Panaroo will not merge below 70 % amino-acid identity, Roary below 95 %. Genes that diverge past the floor become separate families however obviously they are the same thing. Those are not a random sample of the genome: they are the loci under diversifying selection — surface antigens, O- and K-locus biosynthesis, flagellin, fimbrial adhesins, anti-phage defence — the genes anyone studying a pathogen actually cares about.

A Nuna locus is defined differently: a position in the genome that a genome must fill, whose occupants may share no detectable sequence homology at all. Where an identity-threshold tool splits divergent families apart, this groups them by position — so a locus here may hold several families an alignment would never join.

Whether that is better described as one locus with several alleles or as several genes is the open question, and it is the reason for the evidence on each page: how far apart the families are, whether their domain architectures agree, and how consistent the neighbourhood is. The browser is for inspecting that, not for asserting it.

Synteny only means no homology mmseqs, Pfam or ESM could find — the members were grouped on genomic context. That names the evidence, not a mistake: many are good calls, and the way to tell is the evidence beside each one. Clusters rescued by Pfam or by ESM are homologous and are not counted here.

Every locus grouped on context alone (10)

No sequence, Pfam or ESM homology behind the grouping — genomic context is all there was. Worth looking at every one: this is where the model is doing something an identity threshold cannot, and also where it would go wrong. Each is one click away.

Every Pfam conflict (42)

The annotated members carry domain architectures with no clan in common — evidence against the merge, but not a verdict: an HMM can miss homology ESM sees, and a locus whose members are all phage targets or all pilus variants is expected to look varied. A different domain order and a missing domain are both excluded before this count. Judge each against its synteny and its ESM pair.
